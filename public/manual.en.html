1. What is Rivalines?

Rivalines is a multiplayer transit and city-building game that runs in your browser. You run a transport company: lay roads and tracks and open bus, train and air routes. Passengers gather around your stations and stops, and towns grow. In the same world, other players' companies compete with you for passengers and land.

  • There is no fixed win or loss. Compete in the market-value ranking, grow your own towns, or just play as you like.
  • The world is shared. Other players' routes and facilities appear on the same map.
  • No saving needed. The server saves automatically. Your company stays when you log out.
  • While you are logged out, your company is paused (vehicles do not run, and income and costs do not move). While nobody is logged in, the whole world is paused.
  • Time runs at one game day per 60 real minutes (this can change with the server settings).

2. Your first 10 minutes

Sign up and enter

  1. On the top page (https://rivalines.com/) choose your language (it is picked automatically from your browser).
  2. Sign up with a name (2–16 characters) and a password (6 or more characters). No email address or real name is needed. Your name is visible to other players, so do not use your real name.
  3. Choose Live (the world everyone plays in). "Next version" is a world for trying new features, and its data may be erased.

Your first bus route (the easiest way to play)

You start with ¥30,000,000, near a town. First, make one bus route using the roads in a town.

  1. Lay a road. From the toolbar choose "🛣 Road ▾ → 🛣 Road (R)", then drag on the map from the edge of a town to near another town or facility. Connect to the town's existing roads and you can use them as they are.
  2. Place two bus stops. With "🚏 Bus stop (B)", click two places on a road (places with many buildings are best).
  3. Place a depot. With "🏭 Depot", click on a road.
  4. Make a route. In the "Routes" panel on the right, press "＋🚌", click the bus stops in order, and press "Confirm (Enter)".
  5. Buy a bus. Click the depot you placed, choose a model and the route, and press "Buy" (you can also buy with "＋Vehicle" in the Routes panel).
  6. Watch. The bus starts running and passengers get on and off. If your cash at the top goes up, it works. Income floats above the screen as a green "+".
If it does not work, see "14. Troubleshooting". Most "the bus does not move" and "no passengers" problems are: a stop is off the road, a route has fewer than two stops, or the depot is not connected.

Next steps

  • Add more stops and extend the route (you can also make timetables, for example every 8 minutes → chapter 6).
  • Expand to rail and air (chapters 5 and 6).
  • Build hotels and department stores near stations and earn from passengers (chapter 8).
  • Use loans, dividends and training in the Finance screen (chapter 9).

Roads

  • Road (R): drag to lay it. Height differences of one level are joined with slopes.
  • One-way (O): makes a road passable only in the direction you drag. Back to two-way (T) removes it.
  • Bridge / tunnel (Y): drag between both ends to cross rivers and hills.
  • Bus stop (B): click on a road to place it.
  • Depot: click on a road, a straight track, or an airport hangar (or terminal). This is where you buy vehicles.

Tracks and stations

  • Track (L): drag to lay it. Drag at about 45 degrees to lay it diagonally.
  • Auto-lay track: drag from a start to an end and it connects them by the shortest path, using existing track and avoiding obstacles and height differences. Handy for long distances.
  • Station (K): choose the number of tracks, platform length (tiles), design and direction (east-west / north-south), then click. The click is the top-left corner, and track inside the station is laid automatically. Connect the track outside the station yourself. You can see a preview of each design.
  • Waypoint: place on a straight track. Add it to a route and trains pass along that track. It is also used to specify which platform to use or where to wait for another train.
  • Signal (N) and Signal line: signals divide track into sections (blocks) so trains do not get too close. "Signal line" drags along track and places one-way signals at regular intervals.
  • Bridge / tunnel (H): for rail.
  • Depot (rail): place on a straight track.

Airports

"🛫 Airport" goes on flat, empty ground. Choose a size (small, medium, large or hub) a design (1 to 10) and a direction (east-west or north-south) (the dice picks one at random). Designs differ in where the terminal, hangars, stands and runways are; the spot you click is the center of the airport. Before you place it, you see the layout and its cost, number of runways and number of stands.

  • An airport is made of runways, taxiways, stands, a terminal and hangars. The terminal (the blue tile) is the stop that routes use. The hangar is where the depot goes.
  • Make air routes by clicking airports.
  • A small airport cannot take big aircraft: small airports take small aircraft only, medium ones up to medium, and large airports and hubs take all.
  • It cannot be placed on uneven ground. Level it first with the terrain "Level ground" tool.

Aircraft on the ground, and holding

  • An aircraft moves from the hangar to a stand and loads passengers there. To depart it taxis along the taxiways to the end of a runway, accelerates and takes off. After landing it taxis along the taxiways to a free stand.
  • A runway takes one aircraft at a time (a takeoff or a landing). Two aircraft cannot land at the same time.
  • An aircraft that has to wait for the runway or a stand flies circles near the airport (a holding pattern) and comes down when it is free. Many aircraft circling is a sign that the airport is too small for the traffic.
  • A stand takes one aircraft. If all stands are taken, a new arrival cannot land and keeps circling.
  • Large airports and hubs have two runways, so they can handle a takeoff and a landing at the same time.
  • The old 3×3 airports keep working as before (aircraft fly straight from terminal to terminal).

Terrain

"⛰ Terrain ▾" has Raise ground (U), Lower ground (J) and Level ground (G) (matches the area to the height of the first tile you click). The dragged area is affected.

Demolishing

  • "🧹 Demolish (X)" removes only the kinds of elements you choose within the dragged area: roads, tracks, signals, stations/stops, depots, waypoints, bridges/tunnels, town buildings, airports (there are also buttons such as "All transport" and "Roads only").
  • If the area includes stations, stops, waypoints, depots, facilities or airports, a confirmation window appears first. "Cancel" is selected by default.
  • Other companies' things and public facilities of the town (city hall, schools, sights) cannot be demolished.

6. Routes and vehicles

Making a route

  1. In the Routes panel on the right, press ＋🚌 (bus), ＋🚆 (rail) or ＋✈ (air).
  2. Click stops of the same kind (bus stops, stations, airports) on the map in the order they are visited. Bus stops (B), stations (K) and airports (P) are also placed if you click at an empty place while creating a route.
  3. Press "Confirm (Enter)" to finish (or "Cancel (Esc)"). You need at least two different stops.

Each player can have up to 300 routes.

Orders (stopping conditions)

Each stop on a route has an order. Vehicles stop and leave according to it.

  • Interval (stop): stops for the given number of seconds, then leaves.
  • Wait until full: waits until the vehicle is full (with a maximum wait time).
  • Timetable: leaves at a set time, given as a time of day and a cycle.
  • Pass: does not stop at that station and runs through (rapid/express).
  • Conditional jump: jumps to another order depending on conditions such as load, waiting passengers or time.
  • You can reorder (▲▼), insert (＋) and delete (✕) orders.

Train operations (rail)

For trains stopped near a station, use the Info panel.

  • Split: detaches the given number of cars from the rear. The detached train waits.
  • Couple: joins two neighboring trains.
  • Depart: sends a waiting train off.

Timetable (train diagram)

In "📈 Timetable" you see timetables as a diagram and edit them by dragging: 🚆 Move trains (shifts that train's departure times) or 🕒 Move station times (changes that station's departure time). Passing-loop suggestions finds places where overtaking happens and lets you put that wait into the timetable with "Apply".

Vehicle info and following

Click a vehicle to see its route, state (running / stopped / waiting), load and next stops. Press "📍 Follow" and the camera follows it. If it is waiting at a red signal, that is shown.

Letting others use your stops

Another company's stop cannot be used in a route without the owner's permission. In your stop's Info panel, use "🔑 Allow other companies" to allow a company (or all your stops). Running on another company's track or road costs tolls (chapter 11).

7. Passengers and towns

Where do passengers come from?

  • Passengers are born in a stop's catchment area (the buildings and population around it). A stop with more people and closer buildings gets more passengers.
  • A passenger decides a destination and waits at the stop. When a route that can take them arrives, they board (the number waiting is shown at the stop).
  • If one route cannot reach the destination, passengers transfer. The bigger your connected network, the more places they can reach.
  • The fare is a base fare when boarding plus a distance fare when getting off.

Town growth

  • Towns grow by population: village → town → city → metropolis → capital. Towns with good service grow and new buildings appear.
  • Buildings go through a construction period (2–3 days) before they are finished. An under-construction look is shown.
  • As towns grow, private department stores, hotels and malls appear; these can be offered for sale or acquired.
  • Hospitals speed up the growth of nearby towns.

Area development

From "Area development" in Analysis (or a stop's info), you can raise the development level of your stops (max 3). Each level adds 15% more passengers and brings daily real-estate income.

Weather, holidays and events

The weather (sunny, rain, snow and so on) changes daily and there are holidays. Each facility draws more or fewer visitors depending on rain, snow, holidays and season. On event days such as concerts or exhibitions, demand at that facility is several times higher.

Company rating

The company rating is a service-quality score made from coverage (share of population served), few waiting passengers, no congestion and punctuality (it starts at 65). At "Worst" (below 15), you cannot start new developments (you can still demolish, edit routes and buy vehicles). Reduce waiting passengers and congestion and get back to 25 or more to lift it. The rating also affects your credit (loan terms).

Selling and acquiring

  • Sell: at 60% of the appraised value (a sold facility becomes a property for sale).
  • 🏘 Real estate: buy for-sale facilities that no company owns, at appraised value. If no one buys within 28 days, it is demolished.
  • Private facilities built by towns, and rivals' facilities, can be acquired (Finance screen / facility info).

🏷 Bids & monopolies

The exclusive right per town and facility type is auctioned (3 days to close; a new auction every 2 days). Only the winner can build that type of facility within the town's range (a radius set by the town's size). You pay when you win (if you lack the cash, the auction fails). Owners and winning bids are listed on the screen.

9. Money and your company

Open "💴 Finance (F)" (or click the cash at the top).

The flow of money

IncomeCosts
Fares (bus, rail, air), facility sales, subsidiary profit, asset sales, tolls received, funds received, real-estate income (area development), government subsidyConstruction, vehicle purchases, vehicle running costs, facility and station upkeep, interest, acquisitions, corporate tax, dividends, tolls paid, funds sent, advertising, staff training

Changes float briefly below your cash (green + for income, red − for costs such as construction). The Finance screen shows today, the total and recent trends.

Loans

The "Borrow" buttons let you borrow ¥1,000,000, ¥5,000,000 or ¥20,000,000 at a time (there are Repay buttons too). The limit and the annual rate depend on your credit grade (AAA to D). The grade comes from your company rating and net worth; for example AAA is 3% a year, and D is 16% with no borrowing.

Settlement, corporate tax and dividends

  • Settlement is at the end of the fiscal year (end of March); the first is on March 31, 2027. Sales, operating profit, pre-tax profit, tax and net income are listed.
  • The corporate tax rate is 30% (losses are offset against later profits). National policy changes it (chapter 10).
  • Choose a payout ratio (0%, 20%, 40%): the share of net income paid as dividends, which slightly boosts the share price.
  • The share price comes from net worth, profit and credit, and you can see its history in a chart.

Staff training

Raising the training level (max 5) lowers running costs by 4% per level and raises passengers by 3% per level. Training costs money and takes 7 days, and a training fee that depends on the level is charged daily.

Staff (hiring, advertising, education)

Vehicles, stations, airports, depots and your facilities each need a number of employees (1 per bus, 2 plus the cars for a train, 4 to 14 per aircraft, 8 for a small airport up to 120 for a hub, and so on). The Finance screen shows your staff, the number needed, skill and staffing.

  • Wages: every employee costs a wage each day.
  • Hiring and advertising: choose a daily budget for each. More advertising brings more applicants, and they are hired within the hiring budget (a fee per hire). Hiring stops once you have enough staff.
  • Education: raises skill. New hires start with low skill, and skill slowly falls over time.
  • When short: vehicles slow down and passenger numbers and facility sales fall (they never stop completely). High skill lets fewer people do the job.
  • When this was introduced, everything you already had was given exactly the staff it needs, free, on the first day. What you add later needs hiring.

Partnerships (through-running agreements)

When you agree with another company, the tolls for using each other's tracks and roads are discounted (you choose 25%, 50%, 75% or 100%). The proposer sets the discount, and it is formed when the other side accepts. Either side can end it.

Acquiring rival companies

The world has AI rival companies. From the "Rivals (can be acquired)" list you can buy one at its company value. After that, its private facilities become yours, and its profit comes in daily as "subsidiary profit". You can also sell it off at 80% of the company value.

Sending funds

You can send funds to another company from the Finance screen. But using multiple accounts or moving funds to gain an advantage in rankings or auctions is prohibited (chapter 13).

10. The government

This mechanism keeps early players from staying ahead forever. It exists in the multiplayer world.

National policy (monthly)

This month's policy is decided by the date alone (the same for everyone). Click "Government" on the screen to see next month's policy too.

PolicyEffect
Business as usualNothing.
Tax increaseTaxation of big companies doubles. Corporate tax is +5 points for big companies only.
Tax cutTaxation of big companies halves. Corporate tax is −5 points for everyone.
Public worksConstruction costs 20% less.
Tourism promotionFacility visitors increase by 20%.
Environmental regulationVehicle running costs rise (heavier for companies with many vehicles, up to +25%).
DeregulationVehicle running costs are 10% lower.

Administration (yearly)

Each fiscal year (starting in April) an administration with a different character is decided, and it changes how strongly big companies are taxed: business-friendly (0.6×), centrist (1×) or redistribution-focused (1.5×).

Taxing big companies and returning it

  • Online companies are ranked by net worth. The top third are "big companies" and the bottom half are "small companies" (with fewer than 3 companies, nothing is taxed or returned).
  • At month end, big companies pay 0.3% of net worth (changed by policy and administration). The money collected is split equally among small companies (with a cap per company).
  • Companies that are offline (logged out) are neither taxed nor paid.

11. Competing and connecting

🏆 Rankings

There are five: companies (market value = share price × shares outstanding), weekly (net worth gain, passengers, profit), towns (population), stations/stops (boardings) and routes (profit). The weekly ranking is saved and restarted every 7 days, and you can see last week's results.

Dealing with other companies

  • Tolls: running on another company's track or road costs a toll per tile (track ¥300, road ¥80), which goes to the owner. Partnerships discount it.
  • Sharing stops: another company's stops need permission (chapter 6).
  • Auctions, real estate, acquisitions: chapters 8 and 9.

💬 Public chat

"💬 Chat" in the menu opens a chat everyone can see. A message is up to 100 characters, at most 5 messages per 10 seconds, and the same text cannot be sent again within 30 seconds. The log is kept only in the server's memory (the latest 100 messages) and is lost on restart. You can report inappropriate messages.

📢 Announcements

News from the operator appears under "📢 Announcements" in the menu.

Reporting

Report another player's inappropriate name, message or behavior to the operator from "Report" in the player list under "⚙ Settings & Players", with a reason (up to 200 characters). You can report up to 5 times a day, and the same player once a day. The operator reviews and responds.

Statistics, Analysis and List

"📊 Statistics" shows population, passengers carried, the rating breakdown and a list of towns. "🧮 Analysis" shows profit by route (losses first), crowded stops, income and expenses by facility, and area-development candidates. "📍 List" shows towns, equipment and facilities, and vehicles by type; click to jump.

12. Account and settings

Company profile

In "⚙ Settings & Players → 🏢 Company", set your company name (up to 20 characters), profile (up to 120) and company color, and save. The company name and profile are visible to other players. Names that look like the operator or official accounts ("staff", "official", "admin" and so on) cannot be used.

Password and deleting your account

In "🔑 Change password / delete account", enter your current password to change it or to delete your account. Deleting your account erases the account and your company's data (your facilities become properties for sale, and your routes and vehicles are removed). It cannot be undone. If you forget your password, the operator can issue a temporary one (contact via GitHub Issues; do not post personal information).

Language

Switch between Japanese and English with "Language" on the login screen and in the menu. It is picked automatically from your browser and your choice is remembered.

Currency (how amounts are shown)

With "Currency" in the menu you can show amounts in one of 32 currencies, such as yen, US dollars, euros, won and yuan. It is picked automatically from your browser's language and region (for example yen in Japan and dollars in the US), and your choice is remembered in this browser.

  • Only the display changes. Everything in the game is traded in yen, and exchange rates do not move, so there is no gain or loss from exchange rates.
  • Amounts are converted with a fixed snapshot of the rates (the menu shows the date and the source).
  • Amounts in this manual are written in yen (¥). If you chose another currency, read the amounts on screen in that currency.

Choosing a world

"🌐 Switch world" lets you choose again between "Live" and "Next version". Your account is shared, and your company data is separate for each world.

Display

You can toggle day/night, stop names and the minimap from the menu or with keys.

Staying logged in

Your login is kept for 14 days (stored in your browser). On a shared computer, log out when you finish.

14. Troubleshooting (FAQ)

The bus does not move

  • Does the route have at least two different stops?
  • Are the bus stops on a road? Is the road broken somewhere?
  • Did you buy a vehicle? A route alone does not run. Buy vehicles at a depot.
  • Is the depot connected by road to the route's stops?
  • You cannot buy without enough cash. You can take a loan in the Finance screen.

No passengers or no income

  • Are there buildings (people) around the stop? Place stops in or near a town.
  • Click a stop and look at the number waiting. If it is zero, no one is in the catchment area.
  • A fare multiplier that is too high lowers passengers.
  • Look for losing routes under "Profit by route" in Analysis.

A train stops or there is congestion

  • Is the track connected outside the station (you connect it yourself)?
  • A train may be waiting at a red signal. Do not put too many vehicles on single track. Building a passing place (waypoint / passing loop) helps.
  • Check whether a route has too many vehicles and reduce them with "−".

"This belongs to another company"

Another company's stop cannot be used without the owner's permission. Ask the owner to use "Allow other companies".

"Not enough funds"

You can take a loan in the Finance screen (the limit depends on your credit grade). If your company rating is "Worst", you cannot start new developments.

I cannot build a facility

  • Does the lot overlap buildings, roads, tracks, bridges, airports, stops or depots?
  • Are the site conditions met (ski resort = high ground, beach = near the sea, station mall = within 4 tiles of a rail station)?
  • Does another company hold the exclusive right for that type in that town?
  • Is your company rating "Worst"?

The page is old or behaves oddly

When a new version is out, a message at the top says "A new version is out. Please reload the page." Reload your browser.

The world is paused

While nobody is logged in, the whole world is paused. It starts moving when you log in. While you are logged out, your company is paused.

15. Glossary

TermMeaning
StopA general word for bus stops, stations and airports: where passengers board and alight.
DepotThe place where you buy and keep vehicles.
RouteA path of service linking stops in order. Vehicles run according to the route's orders.
OrderThe stopping condition at each stop of a route (interval, wait until full, timetable, pass, and so on).
Train (consist)A set of coupled cars on rail.
Catchment areaThe area around a stop that sends passengers to that stop.
BlockA section of track divided by signals. Only one train can be in a block.
PassA setting that runs through a station without stopping.
Passing waitWaiting at a station and so on to let a train ahead go by.
Market valueShare price × shares outstanding (100,000). A guide to a company's size.
Net worthAssets (cash, facilities, vehicles, depots) minus loans.
Credit gradeA rating that decides loan terms: AAA to D.
Exclusive rightThe right, per town and facility type, for only your company to build it (won at auction).
PartnershipA contract with another company that discounts tolls.
National policy / administrationThe government's monthly stance / its yearly character.
Live / Next versionThe world everyone plays in / a world for trying new features (its data may be erased).
